# Fit Check Rubric (template)

> Sanitized version of the rubric this workspace actually runs. Bracketed values are placeholders — the real file has specific numbers filled in for one candidate's actual constraints, which is exactly why this version doesn't.
>
> Run this **before** any resume tailoring. The whole point is to stop investing hours in a role that was never going to work.

---

## Stage 0 — Hard Gates (pass/fail, no scoring)

Any FAIL means the role gets archived or set aside for later. Do not proceed to scoring until every gate is at least resolved (pass, fail, or explicitly flagged unconfirmed).

| # | Gate | Pass condition | Where to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Location eligibility** | Role is genuinely open to hiring in [CANDIDATE'S ACTUAL LOCATION] | The job board's own structured location field and legal entity, never the JD's prose. Generic "remote" language is not enough — confirm the hiring entity actually exists where the candidate is. |
| G2 | **Compensation floor** | At or above [CANDIDATE'S STATED FLOOR] | JD, public comp-benchmarking sources, or a direct recruiter answer. If undisclosed, mark `Unconfirmed` and ask before investing further time — do not guess. |
| G3 | **Employment type** | Matches what the candidate will actually accept (full-time, contract, EOR, etc.) | JD. |
| G4 | **Role is real and open** | Posted or refreshed recently, still live on the employer's own board | The employer's own ATS (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, or similar), not an aggregator copy — aggregators can be stale by months and miss retitles. |
| G5 | **Working-hours overlap** | No expectation of overlap the candidate has ruled out | Almost never stated in the JD. Ask directly: "What's the expected overlap with the core team?" |

**If any gate is `Unconfirmed`:** status becomes `Watching`. Action = ask the specific open question directly (recruiter, careers page FAQ, etc.). Do not tailor materials yet — this is the single biggest source of wasted effort in the whole workflow.

## Stage 1 — Fit Score (100 points)

### A. Domain fit — 30 pts
How close is the role's actual product surface to the candidate's stated target domain(s)?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 27–30 | Dead center of the target domain |
| 20–26 | Adjacent: shares real structural overlap with the target domain |
| 12–19 | General fit for the broader function, but the role's center of gravity is elsewhere |
| 0–11 | Domain the candidate has explicitly deprioritized |

### B. Technical depth demanded — 20 pts
Does the JD reward the specific technical background the candidate actually has?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 18–20 | Explicitly wants deep technical fluency in the candidate's actual strength area, treated as a peer to engineering |
| 12–17 | Technical background preferred, some of the right language present |
| 6–11 | Generalist role, technical depth not emphasized |
| 0–5 | Actively wants a different specialization (design-led, growth-led, etc.) |

### C. Seniority match — 15 pts
Does the scope of the role match the candidate's actual level, honestly assessed?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 14–15 | Squarely the candidate's demonstrated band |
| 9–13 | One band up, but defensible given specific evidence |
| 4–8 | Meaningfully above or below, in a way that changes the day-to-day job |
| 0–3 | Clearly mismatched (junior role for a senior candidate, or vice versa) |

### D. Keyword overlap — 15 pts
Count JD terms that appear in the candidate's evidence file. **Do not count terms the candidate cannot defend under a follow-up question.**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 14–15 | Strong overlap, most core JD terms defensible |
| 9–13 | Solid overlap, most but not all |
| 4–8 | Partial overlap |
| 0–3 | Minimal genuine overlap |

### E. Company signal — 10 pts
Funding stability, org maturity, culture signal, growth stage — whatever is actually checkable from public sources.

### F. Gap exposure — 10 pts
Score **down** for gaps that are hard to bridge in an interview, and score down hardest when a gap sits at the **center** of the role rather than its edges.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | No material gap |
| 6–8 | One bridgeable gap, peripheral to the core of the role |
| 3–5 | Two gaps, or one gap that is central to what the role is actually about |
| 0–2 | A required core capability the candidate genuinely cannot claim |

## Stage 2 — Decision

| Total | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **80–100** | Strong — apply, full tailored materials |
| **65–79** | Good — apply |
| **50–64** | Stretch — apply only if the pipeline is thin |
| **35–49** | Weak — skip, log the reasoning so the role isn't re-evaluated later |
| **0–34** | Drop |

---

## Stage 3 — Output

Every fit check gets written down in the same block, gates first, then score, then a short honest "why," so a rejected or deprioritized role never gets silently re-litigated a month later without anyone remembering it was already checked.

```
### Gates
G1 ... PASS / FAIL / UNCONFIRMED — <evidence>
G2 ... 
...

### Score
A ... __ / 30
...
TOTAL __ / 100

### Verdict
<apply / watch / skip / drop>

### Why
<the strongest angle, the biggest risk, one thing to emphasize>
```

## Anti-patterns this rubric exists to prevent

- **Applying because the title matches.** Title is the weakest signal; read what the role actually does.
- **Tailoring before an open gate is answered.** Sunk cost then drives a worse decision than doing nothing would have.
- **Adding a keyword the candidate can't defend.** It gets past automated screening and then fails at the human round, which is strictly worse than not matching it at all.
- **Re-evaluating a role that was already checked and logged.** That's what the log is for.
